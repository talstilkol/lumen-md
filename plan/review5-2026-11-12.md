## 2. Is the t8 limitation inherent? (Q2)

**In general, yes.** **For the adversary t8 actually models, no, and lane K missed the defense.**

### Why it is inherent in general

B builds its re-key from the keyring it was shown (K1). K1 lists the phone. The provider is the only link between A and B, so it can show B a view with no trace of A's revocation. B can't tell "A hasn't started" from "A's write is being withheld". This is the fork-consistency bound for an untrusted store with no channel between clients (SUNDR, Mazières–Shasha): detection on merge is the best a client can do, and lane K's read-back plus re-revocation does that. Both candidates rely on B *seeing* something A wrote through the provider, so neither helps against a provider that withholds the whole view:

- **Candidate 1, B checks for a revocation intent sealed by A:** the provider withholds the intent, and B can't treat "no intent" as proof of absence.
- **Candidate 2, a sealed append-only revocation log in the chain:** A's entry lives only on K_A, a sibling B never sees. It does help *after* a merge (stickiness, see F2), but it can't stop B's first wrap.

### Why it isn't inherent for t8

t8's provider passes the probe and drops If-Match **on the keyring only**. The manifest CAS still holds. Before A commits its keyring, A freezes the epoch-1 manifest: `frozen: {epoch: 2, at, by: A}`, sealed under S1 and written with If-Match. That freeze *is* candidate 1's "sealed revocation intent", and it is already in band.

B's `rekeyNow` reads that fresh foreign freeze and immediately freezes over it. That happens in the freeze loop at `engine.ts:606-618`, under the rule "A stale freeze … is frozen again". It then re-keys from K1. `round()` treats the same freeze as "a key change in progress" and waits `FREEZE_TTL_MS` on its own clock. `rekeyNow` doesn't.

### The defense

In `rekeyNow`'s freeze loop, when `m.frozen && m.frozen.by !== s.deviceId` and the keyring doesn't list it as aborted, do what `round()` does: record `frozenSeen`, and until `FREEZE_TTL_MS` has passed **on this device's clock**, refuse with "another device is changing keys", not re-freeze. After the TTL, take today's path: call it off or freeze again.

- **Patch:** `$S/rr5/exp/freeze-defense.diff` (8 lines).
- **Proof:** `$S/rr5/r5-7-t8-freeze-defense.exp.test.ts`, which is lane K's k3 t8 run on the patched worktree, passes 3/3. In the lie case the output is `phone's secret right after both re-keys: false | after A's next round: false`, and B gets `PreconditionFailedError`.
- **Harness changes:** B's failure opens the gate that A waits on, and the "A's next round is a fork" assertion is dropped, because there is no fork.
- **No regressions:** lane K's e2eeRekeyIntegrity 15/15 and e2eeThirdReview 10/10 still pass on the patched worktree.
- **A stronger variant:** put A's `revoke` list in the sealed freeze, so B merges A's revocations into its own re-key instead of refusing.

### What stays inherent

A provider that also lies on the manifest, or shows B the pre-freeze manifest, still wins. Spec §9 should name that adversary precisely: "withholds or reorders *every* object", not "ignores CAS".

## 3. Is "an unverified re-key drops the device list" safe? (Q3)

**For confidentiality, yes.**
- The dropped re-key wraps only for the passphrase and `enroll`.
- `prior.revoked` is carried over.
- Served a stale epoch, it gives the revoked phone nothing: k3 t5 3/3, and `rr5/r5-4` (a) shows no device wrap of the new epoch.

**It is not a new capability for an attacker with the passphrase but no device key.**
- The gates are all on the client. In round 2 the same person got there with `confirmFreshness()` and `rekey({revoke:[all]})`; `r5-4` (b) shows the same lock-out.
- A passphrase holder can also change the passphrase and lock out every other device. That is inherent while the passphrase is the root.
- The owner's passphrase device keeps working and can re-enroll the dropped devices (`r5-4` (c)).

**Not a downgrade.**
- No KDF, protocol or version change follows from it.
- The one downgrade I found, the KDF family on a passphrase change, is separate and comes from the provider (F5).

**The provider can't trigger it on its own.** It needs an honest unverified device whose user starts a re-key.

**The real risks are availability and the user's understanding:**
- **F3:** an honest paired device becomes unverified with no attacker at all, and its next "revoke the lost phone" drops the tablet as well.
- **F4:** the dropped devices are told `RevokedError` ("This device was removed from the vault"), although nobody revoked them.

**Recommendation:** don't drop silently. Have `rekey` refuse on an unverified device unless the caller passes `dropDevices: true` after the UI has shown who will be dropped. Seal a `dropped` marker, so the devices left out get a "re-enroll" error rather than "removed".

## 4. The two-way pairing handshake (Q4)

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| **Reflection** | **Broken (F1).** |
| **Replay** | OK. An answer is bound to the request's 16-byte nonce. The new device keeps one outstanding request and deletes it on success. Answers to older requests don't match. |
| **Nonce binding** | OK for answers. But the answer doesn't echo the request's view (ring or manifest digest). Only `pair()`'s epoch/rev comparison protects that, and it is enough today. |
| **5-minute clock on the new device** | Wall-clock (`Date.now()`, persisted). It is never compared with another device's clock, so skew doesn't matter. The answerer's lock (answer time + 5 min, its own clock) covers the new device's window in real time. **Residue (info):** if the new device's clock is moved back (by the user, or by NTP spoofing) after it makes the request, its acceptance window extends past the answerer's lock. |
| **Third-device window** | A revocation made on a *third* device inside the 5 minutes still slips through. Spec §9 says so; inherent to a snapshot handshake. |
| **Lock side effect (info)** | `pairingUntil` blocks the answering device's own urgent revocation for up to 5 minutes after it answers. The UI needs "cancel pairing" to clear it. |

## New findings

### F1 — MEDIUM — the pairing request reflects as an answer or a one-way code

- **Where:**
  - `src/sync/e2ee/pairing.ts:194` (`encode`) and `:116`: `HMAC(keys.pairing, json)`. The prefix (`lumen-pair-request:1:`, `lumen-pair-answer:1:`, `lumen-pair:2:`) isn't in the MAC.
  - The request body (`:224`) has every field `readPairingAnswer` (`:269-284`) and `readPairingCode` (`:121-157`) check, including the same `nonce` and, for codes, `issuedAt`.
  - `engine.ts:848-877` (`pair`).
- **Scenario:**
  1. A revokes the phone (epoch 2).
  2. The provider, with the phone's help, or a thief who has the phone's storage credentials and old copies, shows a new laptop the epoch-1 snapshot.
  3. The laptop shows its request. The user moves it to the existing device and back by copy and paste: messenger, mail or notes. It is base64 in a textarea, with no QR code.
  4. An attacker in that channel, who needs no key, swaps the prefix to `lumen-pair-answer:1:` (or `lumen-pair:2:`) and returns it as "the answer".
  5. The laptop becomes `paired`.
  6. Its next round uploads under epoch 1, and the revoked phone reads it. Its re-key wraps the next epoch for the phone.
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-1-pairing-reflection.test.ts`. The control passes (A refuses the honest request as `older`). Both reflections FAIL:
  ```
  pair -> ok | freshness: paired | uploaded: 1 | phone (S1) reads: ["a.md","new.md"] | laptop re-key devices: ["phone"] | phone unwraps next epoch: true
  ```
  This holds on #399, #291 and #472.
- **Fix:**
  - **Domain separation:** MAC `utf8("lumen-pair/<kind>/<ver>\0") ‖ json`, put `kind` inside the JSON, and have each reader require its own kind.
  - **Distinct shapes:** give the answer a field the request can't have (`answeredAt` is already there: require it). Make the code's TTL field differ from the request's.
  - **Bind the answer to the request:** include `sha256(request code)` in the answer and check it.
  - **Versions:** bump `request:2` / `answer:2` / `pair:3`, and add vectors.

### F2 — MEDIUM — re-revocation only repairs a sibling at the same epoch

- **Where:** `engine.ts:696`. `reapplyRevocation` returns quietly when `k.file.epoch !== r.epoch`. Line 708 also needs the live keyring's chain to contain `r.parent` exactly as its direct lineage.
- **Scenario:**
  1. t8's lie race happens: B's K_B at epoch 2 wraps the phone.
  2. Before A's next round, B (honest, paired) re-keys once more, for example to rotate or to revoke something else. That makes epoch 3 from K_B, wrapped again for `['phone','laptop']`.
  3. A's next round, and every round after it: `KeyringForkError` with `revocationReapplied: []`. A is wedged, and the UI shows the raw English message.
  4. B, the laptop and the phone see no error. The phone opens epoch 3 with its own key and reads B's later notes (`["a.md","b-later.md"]`).
  5. The revocation that `rekey()` reported as `confirmed: true` is gone for good.
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-2-rerevoke-epoch-moved.test.ts`. The control (lane K's same-epoch case) passes. The epoch-3 case FAILs. Same on all three heads.
- **Fix:**
  - **Generalise the repair:** whenever A has a `rekeyed` record with revocations and sees a fork, open the live keyring with its passphrase access. If its chain descends from `r.parent` at **any** epoch ≥ `r.epoch`, and its sealed `devices` still list any of A's revoked ids or keys, re-key on top of it minus those.
  - **Make revocations sticky locally:** persist A's revoked ids and keys in `DeviceState`, so any keyring that A later adopts or pairs into is checked against them. This is candidate 2, applied after the merge.
  - **UI:** map every `KeyringForkError` within N days of a revocation to `e2ee.rekey.unconfirmed`-style text.
  - **Prevention:** the Q2 defense removes the precondition for t8's adversary.

### F3 — MEDIUM (availability; no attacker) — demotion after 100 commits holds uploads and turns a revocation into a silent drop

- **Where:**
  - `engine.ts:1002-1006`: when the anchor isn't in the manifest's history, the device is demoted to `unverified`. `HISTORY_LENGTH = 100`, set at `manifest.ts:74`.
  - `engine.ts:481-484`: the same happens after 64 keyring revisions.
  - Since round 3, `engine.ts:1140-1149` holds every upload while unverified, and `engine.ts:585` sets `dropDevices`.
- **Scenario:**
  1. A laptop is paired.
  2. The phone makes 101 commits while the laptop is closed. That is one day of normal editing at one commit per sync round.
  3. On the laptop's next sync, `freshness` goes from paired to unverified, and the laptop's edit waits: `uploaded 0, waiting ["l.md"]`.
  4. The user then revokes a lost phone from the laptop. The result is `devices [] dropped true`, so the tablet is un-enrolled and sees `RevokedError`.
  5. Users will learn to click "confirm", which empties the banner of meaning.
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-3-demotion-drops.test.ts` FAILs on all three heads.
- **Fix:**
  - **Don't demote on manifest-history "unproven"** when the secret-bound keyring anchor verified this round. The keyring anchor is what N6 and RR-5 protect. Re-anchor on the new manifest, and use a skip-list or Merkle history if manifest continuity has to be proven over longer gaps.
  - **Never drop silently:** see Q3.

### F4 — LOW — devices left out by a `dropped` re-key are told they were revoked

- **Where:** `keyring.ts:597` (`dropDevices`) and `:418-421` (`checkDeviceEnrolled` → `RevokedError`).
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-4-dropped-rekey.test.ts` (a) FAILs: `tablet opens: RevokedError`. Cases (b) and (c) pass; they show that the capability is not new and that recovery works.
- **Fix:** seal `dropped: {at, by}` in the contents. A device that isn't listed in a keyring with `dropped` gets a `DroppedError` ("a device that couldn't verify the device list re-keyed: enroll this device again"). The `revoked` list stays the only source of "removed".

### F5 — LOW — the provider picks the KDF family of the next passphrase

- **Where:** `engine.ts:413-421` (`kdfOf`) parses the header that `provider.get(HEADER_NAME)` returns at `:593`, without checking its MAC. `:596` then picks PBKDF2.
- **Scenario:** the provider returns `{"kdf":{"alg":"pbkdf2-sha256"}}` for that one read. An Argon2id vault's new passphrase is protected by PBKDF2-SHA256 at 600,000 iterations, which is the floor, and the provider keeps the new header and can guess against it offline. This is pre-existing since RR-10's fix and only matters for passphrases a user chose; the generated 96-bit words make it moot.
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-5-kdf-downgrade.test.ts` FAILs: `created with argon2id; after the passphrase change: pbkdf2-sha256 (600000 iterations)`.
- **Fix:** take the family from the header this device unlocked (keep its `kdf` in memory or in state), or verify the fetched header's MAC with `keys.header` before trusting it. In doubt, use Argon2id.

### F6 — LOW — `pair()` re-seals the anchor with unverified keys

- **Where:** `engine.ts:848-877`. Unlike round, rekey, enroll, `pairingRequest` and `answerPairing`, `pair()` doesn't run `refresh()` / `verifyAnchor` first. At `:872` it seals the code's ring anchor with `this.o.keys.secret`.
- **Scenario:** N1's forgery, with the phone opening by its own key without `state`. `unlockWithDevice`'s `state` is optional, and #291's session doesn't pass state either. Then the user enters a code the provider made under the forged pairing key. The anchor is replaced, and from then on N1's check passes. In the test, the provider reads `new.md` and the phone shows the provider's `a.md`.
- **Reach:** this needs social engineering, and device-key unlock isn't wired in any UI yet.
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-6-pair-reseal.test.ts` FAILs on all three heads.
- **Fix:** start `pair()` with `await this.refresh()`, or with `if (s.ring) await verifyAnchor(this.o.keys, s.ring)`. Never seal an anchor from keys that haven't passed the existing one.

### F7 — LOW (N5 residue) — a forged keyring still gives "This device was removed"

- **Where:** `engine.ts:309` (`checkDeviceEnrolled`) runs before `:311` (`verifyAnchor`).
- **Test:** `rr5/r5-8-forged-revoked.test.ts` FAILs: the result is `RevokedError` even with the device's saved state.
- **Fix:** swap the two lines. With saved state, a keyring that fails the anchor is a `KeyringForkError`, whatever its list says.
