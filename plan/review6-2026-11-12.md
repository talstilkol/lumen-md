### RR6-469-1 (MEDIUM, confirmed): a socket held over midnight resets the daily counts

**Where:**
- `sync-server/collab/roomQuota.mjs:97`. `admit` resets `rooms` and every bucket whenever `counts.day !== day`, whichever way the day moved.
- The day isn't the Quota's own. The room DO computes it when the socket opens (`worker.mjs:297`), keeps it in the socket's attachment, and passes it in when the socket authenticates.
- `Quota.admit` (`worker.mjs:439-446`) then runs `deleteAll()` on the counter's storage.

**Scenario:**
1. The attacker opens N sockets to N fresh committed rooms before 00:00 UTC, without sending `auth:`. Nothing times out an unauthenticated hibernated socket.
2. After midnight, they use today's 20 rooms.
3. They authenticate one held socket. Today's global and per-address counts are wiped.
4. Their next new room carries today's date, which resets the counts again. Repeat.

**Test:** `rr6/c469/r469-quota.mjs` Q2 FAILS. With 10 held sockets, one address opened **200 more rooms** after its 20. Q1, the control, passes with exactly 20 of 25.

**Fix:**
- The Quota computes `dayOf(Date.now())` itself and ignores the caller's day.
- Never move the day backwards.
- Refuse bucket keys from another day, or have the Quota derive the keys from an HMAC input it computes itself.

### RR6-469-2 (MEDIUM, confirmed): the 1,000-room cap closes the server to new users for 90 days

**Where:**
- `wrangler.toml:30`, `COLLAB_MAX_ROOMS = "1000"`.
- `roomQuota.mjs:102`. The cap is one global count of rooms stored, whatever their size.
- A room lives `RETENTION_DAYS` = 90 after its last write (`worker.mjs:274`, `:337`).

**Scenario:** 50 IPv4 addresses (20 a day each), 13 IPv6 /48s, or one address using RR6-469-1, fill the cap in a day with 1,000 tiny rooms of one write each. After that, every new room on the public server gets `permission-denied`, and the session stays peer to peer. This lasts 90 days, and one write per room renews it.

**Test:** Q3 FAILS. The filler stored 1,000 rooms, and a new user's room was refused.

**Fix:**
- Cap stored **bytes** rather than rooms. The size is known at `save`.
- Add a per-address cap on rooms stored, not only new rooms per day.
- When full, evict the least recently written small rooms that only one device ever opened.
- Give such rooms a shorter retention.

### RR6-469-3 (INFO, confirmed): the committed name tests a password guess offline

- `<name>_k<commit>` lets anyone who sees the server-side name test a password guess offline: Q5 found `board2026` from the name alone, at about 90 ms per guess.
- The cost is the same 200,000-iteration PBKDF2 as before. But before, only the server held the verifier. Now the name is in every `?room=` URL.
- It matters only for typed passwords (`localStorage["lumen.collab.password"]`, `yjs.ts:238`); the default is 128-bit random.
- This is inherent: any commitment the server can check gives the same test to anyone who sees it.
- **Fix:** refuse weak typed passwords, keep Workers Logs off (they are), and document it.

### RR6-451-1 (MEDIUM, confirmed in Chromium): frames sandboxed without scripts keep sending

**Where:**
- `src/privacy/airplaneBoot.ts:125-135`. `blankFrames` skips any frame sandboxed without `allow-scripts`.
- `airplaneCertificate.ts:31` justifies that: "it cannot start a request of its own". It can, because CSS needs no script.
- **LiveCssBlock:** `sandbox="allow-forms"` (`LiveCssBlock.tsx:96`), with the user's CSS in a srcdoc that has no CSP of its own (`:48`).
- **HtmlPreviewBlock** with scripts off and a full-document source. `wrapHtml` returns an `<html>…` source as it is, without its `<meta>` (`HtmlPreviewBlock.tsx:163`).
- Both keep the policy they were created with, and the app's header has `img-src … https:`.

**Scenario:** a Live CSS block, or a pasted HTML page with scripts off, is on screen when the user turns airplane mode on. Then:
- an `@keyframes` animation over `background-image` URLs keeps making requests to another site for as long as it runs, with no interaction;
- `:hover` images load as the pointer moves.

Nothing is recorded, and the certificate reads zero.

**Test:** `rr6/r451-escapes.mjs`, using the real `enableAirplaneMode` and an app page served with a CSP shaped like the header:
- **E1** (Live CSS): FAILS, 10 requests in 2.5 s after the switch.
- **E2** (HTML preview, scripts off, full document): FAILS, 10 requests.
- **E4** (`:hover`): FAILS, 1 request.
- **E3** (control: a fragment source, which gets its own `<meta>`): passes, 0.
- **E9** (control: a frame made after the switch): passes, 0.

**Fix:**
- Blank every iframe that has content, whatever its sandbox. Only `about:blank` and empty frames should stay.
- Give the Live CSS and full-document HTML preview srcdocs their own `<meta>` CSP.
- Delete the "cannot start a request" sentence.

### RR6-451-2 (LOW, confirmed): what an embed started outside its frame survives

- **E7:** a popup that an embed opened before the switch keeps sending: 13 requests. `EmbedBlock.tsx:289` grants `allow-popups`.
- **E8:** a service worker of the embed's origin keeps fetching while its `waitUntil` runs: 10 requests in 2.5 s.
- In both cases the frame is recorded as `in-flight`, but the certificate says "stopped at that moment".
- **Fix:**
  - Drop `allow-popups` from embeds, or record popups.
  - Say in `notCounted` that windows an embed opened, and third-party service workers, may keep running after the switch.

### RR6-451-3 (LOW, confirmed, latent): frames `blankFrames` can't see

- `blankFrames` searches only `document.querySelectorAll("iframe")`, and only frames with a `src` or `srcdoc` attribute. Two kinds escape:
  - **E5:** a frame in a shadow root keeps sending (13 requests).
  - **E6:** a frame with no `src` attribute, navigated by script, keeps sending (12).
- I found no such frame in `src/` today: no `attachShadow`, no `contentWindow.location`. But the PR claims the switch covers frames "a plugin adds".
- **Fix:**
  - Blank every iframe, including those with an empty or `about:blank` `src`.
  - Walk open shadow roots, and also stop `<object>` and `<embed>`.
  - State that closed shadow roots are out of reach.

### RR6-473-1 (MEDIUM, confirmed in Chromium): Markdown structure makes the preview shade what the export keeps

**Where:** `src/lib/redaction.ts:197-335`, `markInertCloses`.
- It tracks only tags written as HTML.
- The preview runs Markdown first (`pipeline.ts`: remark, then remark-rehype, then rehype-raw/parse5).
- A `<redact>` on its own line opens an HTML block. The list items, block quotes, table cells and headings after it become `li`, `blockquote`, `td`, `h1` and `p` elements, which are "special".
- HTML's tree builder ignores a `</redact>` written inside one of them. That is the rule at `:228`, but the export applies it only to tags that were written as HTML.
- So the preview keeps shading, while the export ends the passage at that close and publishes the rest.

**Natural case** (`rr6/r473-md-natural.mjs` N1):

```
Interview notes.

<redact>

- Source A: Dana Levi</redact>
- Source B: Yossi Cohen, 054-0000000
```

The preview shades both sources. The export is `█████\n- Source B: Yossi Cohen, 054-0000000`. A block quote (N2) and a table (N3) leak the same way.

**Fuzz with a different generator:** `rr6/r473-md-fuzz.mjs`.
- It builds notes from Markdown blocks: paragraphs, headings, quotes and lists nested two deep, fences and indented code inside containers, math, tables and HTML blocks. Marks go in random places.
- The preview side is micromark (CommonMark + GFM table + math, raw HTML allowed), then **real Chromium's DOMParser**.
- The export side is the worktree's `prepareForExportSync`.
- **Results:** 34 of 3,000 cases leak at seed 6, 61 of 4,000 at seed 11, and 47 of 4,000 at seed 12. The minimised leaks are in `rr6/r473-md-fuzz.leaks.json`.
- There are two classes:
  - closes inside Markdown containers, as in N1–N3;
  - closes inside code in containers that the mask misses, for example a fence or indented code inside `- 1.` or `> -`.

**History:** this was there before. On 2e7f8c632, N1–N4 all leak. ed25db883 fixed N4 (a fence in a list item) but not N1–N3. Either way, it falsifies the PR's claim that the preview shades a subset of what the export removes. The PR's fuzz feeds the raw note to DOMParser and never builds Markdown structure.

**Not run:**
- parse5 itself. Running a parse5 bundle built from a cloned source was refused by the session's permission policy (code from outside).
- jsdom, which isn't available offline.

The oracle is Chromium, which implements the same WHATWG tree builder. micromark stands in for remark-rehype + rehype-raw: both hand parse5 the Markdown-made elements as tags in order.

**Fix:**
- Decide closes on the Markdown tree that the preview uses. `remark-parse` is already a dependency and runs in the CLI. A close should count only if no block node other than the HTML block or paragraph that holds the open lies between them.
- Or, conservatively: a close after a blank line, or on a container line (`>`, a list marker, `|`), is inert.
- Add this generator to the fuzz, through remark-rehype + rehype-raw under jsdom.

## #520 (dictation sent to the desktop), 15e65407 (added by the coordinator)

### The three questions

**Is `svc.*` reachable before the proof?** No.
- `handleService` refuses with `auth` until `c.authed`, and with `locked` without keys (`rpc.ts`).
- **V1:** status and transcribe are refused before the proof, and the engine runs 0 times.
- **V2:** a peer that fails the proof (another passphrase) is refused.
- Services run outside the per-connection chain. A `svc.` request that races the proof is refused, which fails closed.

**Does the sealing bind the request id and the direction?** Yes.
- The AAD is `lumen-e2ee/<fmt>/<kind>/<vaultId>/<rid>` (`keys.ts:176`), with kind `dictation-audio` or `dictation-text`.
- **V3:** the phone's sealed audio doesn't open as text, the text doesn't open as audio, and a reply for request A doesn't open as request B.
- The client also checks `r.header.rid === rid`.
- The vault blob key is reused here, separated by kind, which is fine with random nonces.

**Can a LAN peer use the desktop as an oracle, or exhaust it?** Only a proven peer, which means one of the user's own devices, or anything that knows the passphrase.
- **V4:** a burst of 10 runs 4 and answers `busy` to the rest.
- Two low gaps remain: RR6-520-1 and RR6-520-2.

### RR6-520-1 (LOW, confirmed): abandoned work keeps running

- **Where:** `desktopOffload.ts:283`. A sentence the phone gave up on at its deadline still runs on the desktop. `MAX_QUEUE` counts waiting requests, not live ones.
- **Scenario:** a proven peer keeps sending 30-second sentences and abandons them after 20 ms. The model stays busy, and the user's next sentence times out and falls back to the phone's own text.
- **Test:** V5 FAILS. After 12 abandoned requests, the next sentence got "The hosting device did not answer".
- **Fix:**
  - Send the deadline with the request, and drop queued work past it.
  - Let the peer cancel by request id.
  - Give each peer its own share of the queue.

### RR6-520-2 (LOW, confirmed): oversized payloads are decrypted first

- **Where:** `desktopOffload.ts:275` decrypts the whole payload before it checks that it holds at most 30 s of PCM16. A message may be up to 256 MB (`channel.ts:31`).
- **Test:** V6 FAILS. An 8 MB payload was decrypted before the `too-long` answer: 9.4 MB.
- **Fix:** refuse a sealed length over the 30-second maximum before `unseal`, and refuse a `samples` header over 480,000.

### RR6-520-3 (INFO, from reading): an unknown address passes

`desktopOffload.ts:157` and `:268` let a `null` (unknown) address pass on both ends. The real guard stays W2.19's LAN-only ICE. Either treat `null` as not local for the offload, or say so in the PR.
